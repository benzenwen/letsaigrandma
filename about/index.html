About

"Let's eat, Grandma" is dinner with her. "Let's eat Grandma" is not. One comma.

"Let's AI, Grandma" is the same joke: using AI with Grandma, or doing something to her. The comic lives in that comma.

It follows one family trying to make sense of AI as it moves from novelty into ordinary life. Nana has seen technology hype before and is not impressed by marketing, or by people who dismiss things on principle. Joey is eleven and would like the machine to build him a castle. Jane is in high school and suspects that opting in and opting out are both traps. Mom and Dad have jobs.

The strip is not "AI good" or "AI bad." It is about the absurdities, promises, contradictions, and unintended consequences of AI, seen from a kitchen table. The recurring question is less "what can it do?" than "what are we choosing to hand over, and what happens to us when we do?"

Drawn by hand: pen and ink, opaque watercolor, real paper.
